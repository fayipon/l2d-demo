# HARU改 — the Bench's Test Fixture

## Goal
A model for the Live2D bench to be checked against: Haru with a recoloured
upper garment, living inside `tools/l2d-viewer` and having nothing to do with
project01's roster.

## Why the bench needs one at all

A bench needs something whose answer is already known, so that when the tool
says something surprising the **tool** is the suspect rather than the model.

The four models it serves are not that. They come out of
`project01/public/live2d`, they are the subject rather than the reference, they
belong to the game, and they will change when the game changes. A fixture that
moves when the thing under test moves is not a fixture.

## Decisions

**1. Self-contained, not a one-texture derivative.**

The cheaper design was a folder holding only the recoloured texture, with
`../haru/` for the moc, motions and expressions. It was rejected for a reason
that only applies to a bench: **a real folder can be dragged in.** The
folder-drop path is the tool's headline feature and the only part of it never
exercised by hand — see the note in `FINISH/live2d-model-viewer.md`. A fixture
that is a complete folder makes testing that a drag rather than a hunt for some
model to try.

1.4 MB, 43 files, no parent references anywhere in the manifest.

**2. Generated by a script, output committed.**

`npm --prefix tools/l2d-viewer run fixture`. The recolour is a set of numbers to
tune rather than an image to re-export, which is the same argument
`optimize-assets.mjs` already makes for the backgrounds it hue-rotates. sharp
joins as a devDependency of the tool; `npm run dev` never needs it.

The script reads project01's `DESIGN/` source and shipped model. That is a
*build-time* dependency only — the fixture it writes is self-contained at
runtime, which is what "跟 project01 無關" has to mean for something derived
from Haru in the first place.

**3. The voice clips are left out.**

Drops 645 kB and gains a second test case: with no `Sound` anywhere the emitter
takes its "no motion carries a Sound" branch, which until now only Mao
exercised.

**4. 上衣 is the whole upper garment.**

Vest body, six sleeve and cuff pieces, and the lapel — they are one blazer and
share one colour, and recolouring the vest alone would leave the arms navy and
read as a mistake. **Not** the skirt, the tie, the white blouse or the hair,
all of which share the same atlas.

**5. Crimson, because Haru already is.**

The roster gives her the accent `#ff2b3d` and the title 緋月劍士 — 緋 is crimson
— and neither was anywhere on her.

## Progress

Done. Three things were learnt rather than planned.

**The mask is rectangles, subtractive.** Every rectangle is a drawable's UV
bounding box read off the loaded model — `getDrawableVertexUvs`, scaled by 2048
and flipped, because Live2D's UV origin is bottom-left and an image's is
top-left. Ten are the garment, nine must not move. The rule is: recolour a pixel
in **any** garment rectangle and in **no** keep rectangle, which resolves the
several boxes that touch or overlap by a pixel or two in favour of not touching
what must not move.

The one boundary that would have gone wrong on its own was measured rather than
guessed: the skirt's right edge and the left sleeve's left edge are eight
transparent pixels apart at x=907–914, so the sleeve's rectangle starts at 911
rather than at its true left edge of 893 and cuts neither.

**`sharp`'s `hue` is not an HSL rotation.** +130 on a measured 227 landed at 21
— brown — and not at the 357 the arithmetic promised, because it rotates in a
linear space. The numbers were arrived at by measuring the output and
correcting, twice: 21° was brown, then 358° at 32% saturation still read as
maroon because red at 24% lightness reads brown, and the saturation lift is what
finally made it a colour rather than a tint.

**A second identifier was needed.** The emitter derived one from the label, and
`HARU改` stripped to `[a-z0-9]` is `haru` — a different character that already
exists, whose config the emitted block would have quietly redefined. Sources
carry an explicit `id` now.

### Measured

The build script proves its own claim rather than stating it. Every keep
rectangle is sampled in the source and again in the written output and the drift
is reported; a leak would move one by tens of points where compression costs one
or two.

```
fixtures/haru-kai
  texture_01   1,167,064 pixels recoloured
  vest         hsl(242, 8, 27)  ->  hsl(1, 32, 35)
  27 motions, 8 expressions, no sounds
  keep regions intact (worst drift 1.0 of 6 allowed)
```

The check fails the build rather than warning, because a recolour that has
leaked is not a fixture worth keeping.

`npx tsc -b` clean, and the fixture loads in the bench: moc3 v1, the picker
offers it as HARU改, and the emitted config is named `haruKai`.

### What is worth watching

**The white piping picked up a little warmth.** It sits inside the garment
rectangles and is not transparent, so the saturation lift reached it. At this
strength it reads as warm-white trim on a crimson blazer, which is plausible
tailoring — but it is a real effect and not an intended one, and if the colour
is pushed further it will become pink.

**Drag-and-drop is still untested by hand.** The fixture now makes it a drag
rather than a hunt, which was the point, but nobody has done it yet.

## Out of scope
Nothing in project01 changes. No roster entry, no `live2dConfig.ts`, no arena
loadout, no sprite sheet — this is a bench fixture, not a character.

The recolour changes colour, not shape: the mesh is fixed in the moc, so the
silhouette and the cut of the garment cannot move. See the layers in
`tools/l2d-viewer/README.md`.
